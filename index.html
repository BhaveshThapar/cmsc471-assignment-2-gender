Two views of secondary school enrollment

The same World Bank data can tell different stories about girls' and boys' secondary school enrollment in Sub-Saharan Africa. The first chart is deliberately misleading; the second gives the longer view.

Black hat

How this chart misleads

The red line shows the difference between boys' and girls' gross secondary enrollment rates in percentage points. We subtract the girls' rate from the boys' rate for each year, then show only 1996 through 2009. During that slice, the gap rises from 5.04 to 7.93 points. A reader who sees only these years can reasonably conclude that the disparity kept growing. The source data continue through 2018, when the gap is 5.29 points. Omitting those later years is the main selective transformation.

The vertical axis also starts near the smallest value in the selected period rather than at zero. This stretches a change of a few percentage points across almost the full height of the plot. The tall, narrow frame adds to that impression. A smoothed curve rounds off the year-to-year changes and makes the selected trend look more continuous than the original annual observations. Red color and the title “Is anyone closing the gap?” give the graph an urgent interpretation before the viewer has checked the axis or date range.

None of the underlying enrollment values are invented. The deception comes from the period, scale, curve, and framing. The chart is especially easy to overread because “gap” sounds like an outcome measure, but gross enrollment says how many students are enrolled relative to the population of official secondary-school age. It does not measure graduation, learning, or equal access within every country in the region. The source note below gives the data's origin; the white-hat chart restores the omitted context.

White hat

On a small screen, scroll the chart sideways to see all years.

Why this chart is clearer

The white-hat view plots the female and male gross secondary enrollment rates themselves for every available Sub-Saharan Africa year in our subset, 1970 through 2018. Both lines share a single vertical scale from 0% to 50%, which includes zero and covers all plotted values. The legend names each line directly, and the axes state the year and unit. Hovering over the plot gives both rates and their difference for a selected year. The full span shows rising enrollment for both groups and makes it possible to see that the gap widened during some years and narrowed later. For example, the gap is 5.04 points in 1996, 7.93 in 2009, and 5.29 in 2018.

The local CSV is a two-decimal-place subset of the World Bank gender indicators compiled in the linked repository. It contains Sub-Saharan Africa and World rows, but these charts filter to Sub-Saharan Africa. The white chart uses the female and male secondary-school gross enrollment columns without smoothing or aggregating the years again. The black chart uses those same columns to calculate the male-minus-female gap. The World rows and tertiary enrollment columns are not used in either plot.

These are regional estimates, so they hide differences among countries and depend on the reporting and aggregation choices behind the World Bank series. Gross enrollment can exceed 100% because it counts enrolled students regardless of age. It should not be read as the percentage of children who completed school. The source reports female and male categories; this chart does not describe students outside those categories. These limits matter even when the axis and time range are shown honestly.

Sources and process

Data: World Bank gender indicators CSV. Indicator definitions: female and male gross secondary enrollment. The visualizations use D3.js; they do not adapt an existing chart example.
